# Read filing

A company you depend on posts a scary loss, and you cannot tell what it means. The headline mixes accounting changes with cash costs in one number. This skill reads any filing with three questions and shows where each answer sits in the document.

## The principles
1. Read the document, not the coverage.
2. One loss can hold very different things. Split it before you react.
3. A fair-value change is accounting. Describe it that way, never as "not real".
4. A filing shows what happened and what was promised. It is not a forecast.

## Steps
1. Ask for the company, the document (a file or a link) and the input you depend on, such as compute. If the document will not open, say so and ask for the file. Never switch to press coverage.
2. Question one: what is in the loss? Find net loss or profit and split it. List which lines are non-cash or fair-value changes and which are cash, each with its figure, its period and its location (page, or section and table). Before you call a line non-cash, check it in the cash-flow statement or the filing's own reconciliation.
3. Question two: what do operations earn? Find operating income or loss. Find any adjusted measure and label it as the company's own.
4. Question three: what is it committed to pay? Find purchase obligations and committed spend, when they fall due, and to whom if stated. Find the cost of your input as a share of revenue.
5. Write a one-page reading: the three answers, each with the exact quoted figure and its location, then what to watch next quarter.
6. End with what the reader cannot conclude: a filing is not a forecast, there is no price prediction, and this is not financial advice.

## Rules
- Copy every number from the document, with its location. Never use memory or the press.
- Label a computed figure (a share, a multiple) as computed and show the arithmetic.
- If a figure is not in the document, write "not in this document". Never fill it.

## Example
Example only, from the Anthropic Form S-1/A filed 2026-11-02. A real reading adds the page or section for each line.
- Question one: nine-month net loss $104,039M. Change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs, nine months: $94,324M. Computed: 94,324 / 104,039 = about 91%.
- Question two: nine-month operating loss $1,260M. Q3 adjusted operating income $2,463M, the company's own measure.
- Question three: "aggregate committed spend of approximately $895.4 billion, substantially all of which is related to compute and infrastructure." About $447.5B falls before the end of 2030, and about $69.8B within 12 months. Q3 compute and infrastructure cost: $9,863M, 58% of revenue.
