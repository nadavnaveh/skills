# AI in a business

We added AI to the business, and nobody can say what it saved. It started as a big system, so no single step got faster and nobody measured. This skill maps one process, automates its most repeated step with a small agent, counts the hours and hands the agent to an owner.

## The principles
1. Map before you build.
2. How often a step happens decides what to automate.
3. One task, not a system.
4. Saved hours are measured, not promised.
5. An agent with no owner stops working, and nobody notices.

## Steps
1. Map it. Pick one process, such as invoices. Walk it with the person who does it and write each step on its own line: what it is, who does it, when. Ask "Is any step missing?" and go on after a no.
2. Count. Next to each step, write how many times a month it happens and how long each takes. Use real numbers from last month or a log, and mark any guess as a guess.
3. Pick the repeat: the step done most often by the same rules each time, like "enter the invoice", 200 times a month. Say why in two lines and ask "Automate this one?" Go on only after a yes.
4. Build a small agent for that step only: one short skill that says what it reads, what it does and what it hands back. Run it on one real item, such as invoice 41, and compare it with what a person would enter.
5. Measure the hours. Before: hours by hand last month, for example 20. After: hours people still spend on the step, checking included, for example 3. Say where each number came from.
6. Name an owner: one person on the team who runs the agent and checks a sample of its work every week. Write down who, what the check is and the first date.
7. Run the first weekly check with the owner and report pass or fail with what was checked.

## Rules
- One step per agent. A second step gets its own map, count and yes.
- No "hours saved" without a before number. If there is none, time a week by hand first.
- If the weekly check fails twice, stop the agent and go back to step 4.
- No owner, no launch.
